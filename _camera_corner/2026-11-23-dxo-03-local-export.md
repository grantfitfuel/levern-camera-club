## Local adjustments

1. Open **Local Adjustments** (a palette or tab in the editing panels on recent versions; a toolbar button on older ones)
2. Choose a tool from the list that appears (on older versions, right-click the photograph for a tool wheel):
   - **Control Point:** click on the subject. A circle appears; drag its edge to set the size. PhotoLab finds similar colours and tones within the circle and applies the change only to those. Use the small sliders beside the point, or the panel on the right, to change brightness, contrast, saturation and more. This is DxO's famous U Point technology
   - **Control Line:** like a control point, drawn as a line. Good for skies
   - **Graduated Filter:** drag down from the top for a sky
   - **Brush** and **Auto Brush:** paint where you want the effect. Auto Brush follows edges
   - **Control Brush** (version 10): a control point you paint on, so the selection follows your brush
   - **AI masks:** recent versions select the subject, people (even eyes, lips and skin) and other areas for you. Version 10 adds **depth masks**, which select by distance, such as just the background
3. Use the sliders for that mask (brightness, contrast, saturation and more)
4. Each mask is listed in the Local Adjustments panel; click its eye to hide it

### Dodge and burn

A control point on the subject with brightness raised slightly, and a brush at low opacity with brightness lowered on bright edges, guide the eye. Small amounts.

## Repair

Click the **Repair** tool (the plaster) in the top toolbar. Paint over dust or a small distraction. Choose **Clone** or **Repair** mode; drag the source circle if PhotoLab picks a poor area. Version 10 adds automatic AI dust removal. For big removals, send the photograph to Photoshop, Affinity or GIMP (version 10 has a button to send it straight to Affinity).

## Export

1. Select the photographs in the filmstrip
2. Click **Export to Disk** (at the bottom right of the window, or in the **File** menu)
3. Choose an option or create a new one:
   - **Format:** JPEG, quality around 90
   - **ICC Profile:** sRGB
   - **Resize:** tick it and set the long edge, such as 2000 pixels
   - **Destination folder** and **suffix**
4. Click **Export**

### DNG for another program

To use DxO's noise reduction and lens corrections and then finish in Lightroom, export as **DNG** with only denoising and optical corrections applied (the exact option name varies by version). You get a clean RAW-like file that Lightroom treats like your original. DxO sells the same feature on its own as PureRAW.

Check each competition's rules for size and naming. The Exporting for screen and print lesson goes into more detail.

> Clean files in, careful edits, clean files out.
