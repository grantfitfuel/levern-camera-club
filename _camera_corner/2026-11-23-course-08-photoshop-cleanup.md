These are the two jobs most photographers use Photoshop for: tidying up distractions and shaping the light. Both are done on separate layers, so nothing is permanent.

## Clean-up

Create a new empty layer (**Ctrl+Shift+N**, Cmd+Shift+N on a Mac) and name it Clean-up. With any of the tools below, tick **Sample All Layers** in the options bar so they work on the empty layer.

- **Spot Healing Brush (J).** Paint over a dust spot, a stray twig or a sign and it blends away. Set Type to Content-Aware
- **Remove tool** (in the same group as Spot Healing, press Shift+J to cycle). Paint roughly over a larger object, such as a person or a bin, and Photoshop fills it in
- **Clone Stamp (S).** Alt-click (Option-click) to choose a source, then paint. Slower, but best near edges and patterns, where the others smudge
- **Content-Aware Fill.** Draw round an area with the Lasso (L), then Edit, Content-Aware Fill. Good for bigger patches of sky or grass
- **Generative Fill.** Select an area and type what should be there, or leave it blank to remove. It needs an internet connection and uses Adobe generative credits

Check the rules before entering a cleaned-up photograph in a competition. Many allow removing dust and small distractions but not adding things, and some do not allow generative AI at all.

The Cleaning up and Portrait retouching lessons go into more detail.

## Dodge and burn

1. Create a new layer, then Edit, **Fill**, and choose **50% Gray**
2. Set the layer's blend mode (top of the Layers panel) to **Overlay**. The grey disappears
3. Choose a soft brush (**B**) at about 5 to 10% opacity
4. Press **D**, then **X** to swap: paint **white to dodge** (lighten) and **black to burn** (darken)

Build it up in many light strokes. Lighten the subject and the paths you want the eye to follow; darken bright edges and distractions. Lower the layer opacity if it goes too far.

## Finishing

- Hide each layer (the eye icon) to check it is actually helping
- For effects that need everything merged, use **stamp visible**: Ctrl+Alt+Shift+E (Cmd+Option+Shift+E on a Mac) creates a merged layer on top and keeps the rest
- Save and close to send it back to Lightroom
