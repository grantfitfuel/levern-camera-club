Affinity began as three apps (Affinity Photo, Designer and Publisher). In late 2025 Canva, which owns it, merged them into one app simply called **Affinity**, and made it **free**. You need a free Canva account to sign in. The core editing tools, RAW development, layers and masks are all free; only the **Canva AI** tools (such as Generative Fill and Remove Background) need a paid Canva plan.

It does the job of Photoshop: layers, retouching, combining photographs. It does not organise a library of photographs like Lightroom, so use your computer's folders (or Lightroom, Capture One or DxO) for that, and Affinity for editing.

Anyone with the older Affinity Photo 2 can keep using it. The tips here follow the new app; most of them apply to Affinity Photo 2 as well, where "studios" were called "personas".

## Getting it

1. Go to **affinity.studio** and download the app for Windows or Mac. On an iPad, install **Affinity** from the App Store (the iPad version arrived in November 2026)
2. Install it and sign in with a Canva account (or create one; it is free)

The tips here describe the Windows and Mac version. The iPad version has the same tools, designed for touch and Apple Pencil.

## Studios

Affinity groups its tools into **studios**, chosen from the studio button in the **context toolbar** along the top of the window. For photographs the two that matter are:

- **Develop:** for RAW files. Opening a RAW file puts you here automatically
- **Pixel:** for everything else: layers, retouching, adjustments, painting

There are also ready-made studios such as **Retouching**, **Colour Grading** and **Compositing**, which simply bring the right tools to the front. **Vector** and **Layout** are for design work and can be ignored.

## The layout

- **Left: the toolbar.** One icon per tool. Hover to see the name and shortcut letter. Some icons hide related tools: click and hold, or look for a small triangle
- **Top: the context toolbar.** Shows the settings for the chosen tool, such as brush width and opacity
- **Right: the panels.** **Layers** is the most important. Others include **Adjustments**, **History** and **Histogram**. If one is missing, find it in the **Window** menu
- **Middle:** the photograph

## Opening photographs

Choose **File, Open** (Ctrl+O, or Cmd+O on a Mac), or drag a photograph onto the window.

- A **RAW** file opens in **Develop**. Make the basic tonal and colour corrections there first (the next tip), then click **Develop** to move into the Pixel studio
- A **JPEG** or **TIFF** opens straight into Pixel

## Moving around and undoing

- Use the **zoom** controls in the View menu or toolbar to fit the photograph to the window or see it at 100%
- Hold the **Space bar** and drag to move around
- **Ctrl+Z** (Cmd+Z) undoes. The **History** panel lists every step; drag its slider back to rewind

## Saving

**File, Save** saves an Affinity file (ending .af) with all your layers, so you can come back and change things. When finished, use **File, Export** to make a JPEG (see the third tip).

> Free, powerful and no subscription. Learn a little at a time.
