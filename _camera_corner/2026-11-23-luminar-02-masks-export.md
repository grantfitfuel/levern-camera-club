## Masking a tool

Almost every tool in Luminar can be limited to part of the photograph.

1. Open a tool, such as **Develop**, and make the change
2. Open the tool's **Masking** option
3. Choose how to make the mask:
   - **Mask AI:** Luminar recognises parts of the photograph, such as sky, people, buildings, water or vegetation. Click the one you want
   - **Brush:** paint the area. Set the size, softness and opacity; switch to erase to remove
   - **Radial gradient:** an oval, soft at the edge
   - **Linear gradient:** a gradual change across the frame, like a graduated filter
4. The masked area is highlighted while you work. Click **Done** when finished

To use the same tool twice, with different masks, add a new layer in the **Layers** panel and mask the tools on it.

### Dodge and burn

Use two masks: a **Develop** adjustment with Exposure raised slightly, brushed onto the subject, and another with Exposure lowered slightly, brushed onto bright edges. Some versions also have a dedicated **Dodge & Burn** tool. Build it up gently.

## Removing distractions

- **GenErase** (under **Extras**, may need an internet connection): paint over a person, wire or litter and Luminar fills the area using generative AI
- **Clone:** copies from one area to another for precise work
- Older versions also have an **Erase** tool with automatic dust spot and power line removal

Check the competition rules before entering a photograph with things removed or added. **GenSwap** and **GenExpand** (also under Extras) add generated content, which most competitions do not allow.

## Exporting

1. Click the **Export** button at the top right (or right-click the photograph and choose an export option)
2. **Format:** JPEG, quality around 90
3. **Color space:** sRGB
4. **Size:** set the long edge, such as 2000 pixels, if the size options offer it
5. Choose a folder and file name, then click **Export**

Luminar keeps your edits in its catalog, so you can export again at any size later.

> Remove what distracts. Keep what is true.
