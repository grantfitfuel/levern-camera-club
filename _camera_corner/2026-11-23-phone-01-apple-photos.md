Apple's Photos app has a good set of editing tools, and every edit can be undone, even years later. The steps below are for iPhone and iPad; the Mac version has the same tools in a sidebar.

Apple moves buttons around a little with each version of iOS, so if something is not exactly where described, look for its name or icon.

## Opening the editor

1. Open **Photos** and tap a photograph
2. Tap the **Edit** button (a sliders icon, or the word Edit, depending on version)
3. The editing tools appear in sections: **Adjust**, **Filters**, **Crop**, and on newer iPhones **Clean Up**

## Using a slider

- Tap a tool's icon, such as Exposure, then slide the dial or bar left and right
- **Tap the icon again** to switch that adjustment off and on, for a before and after
- **Tap the photograph** to see the original
- Tap **Done** to keep the changes, or **Cancel** to discard them

## An order that works

### 1. Crop

Tap **Crop**. Drag the corners. The dial straightens; the other two options fix vertical and horizontal perspective. Tap the ratio icon to choose a shape such as square or 4:5. Photos often straightens the horizon automatically.

### 2. Adjust

Work through the **Adjust** sliders roughly in this order:

- **Exposure:** overall brightness
- **Highlights:** slide left to bring back a bright sky
- **Shadows:** slide right to open dark areas
- **Contrast**
- **Black Point:** deepens the darkest areas. A small amount adds richness
- **Brilliance:** brightens dark areas and darkens bright ones at the same time. Powerful; go gently
- **Vibrance** before **Saturation**
- **Warmth** and **Tint:** fix a colour cast
- **Definition:** adds clarity. **Sharpness** and **Noise Reduction** finish it
- **Vignette:** optional

The **Auto** wand at the start applies Apple's own correction. It is a fair starting point; you can adjust any slider after it.

### 3. Filters

Filters are a starting point for a mood, especially the black and white ones (**Mono**, **Silvertone**, **Noir**). Slide to set their strength.

### 4. Clean Up

On iPhones and iPads with Apple Intelligence, **Clean Up** removes distractions: tap or circle the object and it disappears. Check the rules before entering a cleaned-up photograph in a competition.

## Copying an edit to other photographs

With an edited photograph open (not in edit mode), tap the **•••** (More) button and choose **Copy Edits**. Open the next photograph, tap **•••** and choose **Paste Edits**. On the Mac, use **Image, Copy Edits** and **Paste Edits**.

## Undoing

Open the photograph, tap **Edit**, then **Revert** to go back to the original at any time.

## Portrait mode photographs

Photos taken in Portrait mode can have their background blur changed afterwards: tap **Edit**, then the **f** number at the top, and slide. Tap the subject to change the point of focus on newer iPhones.

> The best editor is the one you already have. Learn it properly.
