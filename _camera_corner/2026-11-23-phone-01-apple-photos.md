Apple's Photos app has a good set of editing tools, and every edit can be undone, even years later. The steps below are for iPhone and iPad; the Mac version has the same tools in a sidebar.

Apple moves buttons around a little with each version of iOS, so if something is not exactly where described, look for its name or icon.

## Opening the editor

1. Open **Photos** and tap a photograph
2. Tap the **Edit** button (the sliders icon)
3. The editing tools appear in sections: **Adjust**, **Filters**, **Crop** and, on iPhones with Apple Intelligence, **Clean Up**. Portrait mode photographs have an extra section for the background blur

## Using a slider

- Tap a tool's icon, such as Exposure, then slide the dial or bar left and right
- Tap the **before and after** button (two small boxes, one dark and one light) to switch between your edit and the original
- Tap **Done** to keep the changes, or **Cancel** to discard them

## An order that works

### 1. Crop

Tap **Crop**. Drag the corners. The dial rotates to level the horizon; the vertical and horizontal skew options fix leaning lines. Tap the aspect ratio button to choose a shape such as square or 4:5. Photos often straightens the horizon automatically.

### 2. Adjust

Work through the **Adjust** sliders roughly in this order (there are fifteen; swipe along the row to find them):

- **Exposure:** overall brightness
- **Highlights:** slide left to bring back a bright sky
- **Shadows:** slide right to open dark areas
- **Contrast**
- **Black Point:** deepens the darkest areas. A small amount adds richness
- **Brilliance:** brightens dark areas and darkens bright ones at the same time. Powerful; go gently
- **Vibrance** before **Saturation**
- **Warmth** and **Tint:** fix a colour cast
- **Definition:** adds clarity. **Sharpness** and **Noise Reduction** finish it
- **Vignette:** optional

The **Auto** button applies Apple's own correction. It is a fair starting point; you can adjust any slider after it.

### 3. Filters

Filters are a starting point for a mood, such as Vivid, Dramatic or the black and white ones. Slide to set their strength.

### 4. Clean Up, Extend and Reframe

On iPhones with Apple Intelligence:

- **Clean Up** removes distractions: tap or circle the object and it disappears
- **Extend** (new in iOS 27) adds more picture around the edges, generated by AI
- **Reframe** (new in iOS 27) shifts the viewpoint slightly

All three change what is in the photograph. Most club competitions do not allow generated content, and some do not allow removal either. Check the rules before entering a photograph edited this way.

## Copying an edit to other photographs

With an edited photograph open (not in edit mode), tap the **•••** (More) button and choose **Copy Edits**. Open the next photograph, tap **•••** and choose **Paste Edits**. On the Mac, use **Image, Copy Edits** and **Paste Edits**.

## Undoing

Open the photograph, tap **Edit**, then **Revert** to go back to the original at any time. Edits in Photos are never permanent.

## Portrait mode photographs

Photos taken in Portrait mode can have their background blur changed afterwards: tap **Edit**, open the Portrait section and slide the depth (defocus) control.

> The best editor is the one you already have. Learn it properly.
