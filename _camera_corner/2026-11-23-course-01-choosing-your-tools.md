Adobe's Photography plan gives you three apps with confusingly similar names. They are built for different jobs, and once that is clear, the choice is easy.

## The three apps

- **Lightroom Classic** is the desktop app for computers. Your photographs stay in folders on your own computer or drives, and a catalog file keeps track of them: where they are, how you rated them and how you edited them. It is the most complete organiser and editor of the three
- **Lightroom** (once called Lightroom CC) is the cloud-based app. It runs on phones, tablets, computers and in a web browser, and keeps your photographs in Adobe's cloud so they appear on every device. It is simpler, and the editing sliders are the same as in Classic
- **Photoshop** works on one picture at a time, pixel by pixel, with layers. It does what Lightroom cannot: removing awkward objects cleanly, combining frames, detailed retouching

## Edits that never harm the original

Lightroom Classic and Lightroom are non-destructive. Moving a slider does not change your original file. The app stores your edits as a list of instructions and applies them on screen. You can undo anything, at any time, years later, and the original is always there.

Photoshop changes pixels. Working on layers keeps it reversible, which is why the Photoshop tips here always start with a new layer.

## Which should be your main library?

Pick one app as home for all your photographs, and use the others only when you need them.

- **You shoot mostly with a camera and edit at home on a computer:** Lightroom Classic. It works without the internet, handles very large collections, and your files stay on drives you control
- **You shoot mostly on a phone, or move between devices:** Lightroom. Everything syncs, and you can edit on the train and finish on the computer
- **Either way:** Photoshop is the finishing tool for the few pictures that need more than Lightroom can give

You can have both. Lightroom Classic can sync chosen collections to Lightroom, so you can edit them on your phone or tablet too. Tip 7 shows how.

## The workflow these tips follow

1. **Find your way around** the apps (tip 2)
2. **Import and organise** in Lightroom Classic (tip 3)
3. **Pick your best,** quickly, before you edit anything (tip 4)
4. **Edit the whole picture** in a set order (tip 5)
5. **Edit parts of it** with masks (tip 6)
6. **Edit on the move** with Lightroom on a phone or tablet (tip 7)
7. **Take it into Photoshop** when Lightroom is not enough (tip 8)
8. **Clean up and dodge and burn** in Photoshop (tip 9)
9. **Export** for a competition, the web or print (tip 10)

Most photographs never need Photoshop at all. A good one often needs only a few minutes of editing in tip 5.

## RAW or JPEG?

Shoot RAW if your camera allows it. A RAW file keeps everything the sensor recorded, so white balance, highlights and shadows can be recovered in ways a JPEG cannot. JPEGs can still be edited in all three apps, but with less room before the picture falls apart.

> Choose one home for your photographs. Everything else gets easier from there.
