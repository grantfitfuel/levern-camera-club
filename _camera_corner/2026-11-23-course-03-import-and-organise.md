Lightroom Classic is only as good as its organisation. Ten minutes of setting up now saves hours of hunting later.

## How Classic stores things

Two separate things work together:

- **Your photographs** live in ordinary folders on your computer or an external drive
- **The catalog** (a file ending .lrcat) records where each photograph is, plus every rating, keyword and edit

The catalog does not contain your photographs. If you move or rename folders outside Lightroom, it loses track of them and shows a question mark. Always move and rename from inside Lightroom's **Folders** panel (left side of the Library module).

Use one catalog for everything. The first time you open Lightroom Classic it creates one for you, which is fine. A single catalog comfortably holds hundreds of thousands of photographs.

## Your first import, step by step

1. Put your memory card in the computer (or plug in the camera). Lightroom Classic may open the Import window by itself. If not, go to the **Library** module (press **G**) and click **Import** at the bottom of the left panels, or press **Ctrl+Shift+I** (Cmd+Shift+I on a Mac)
2. **Left side, "From":** click your memory card. If you cannot see it, click the name at the top left and choose it from the list
3. **Top middle:** click **Copy**. This copies photographs off the card. (Use **Add** instead for photographs already on your computer: it leaves them where they are)
4. **Middle:** thumbnails of everything on the card. Ticked ones will be imported. Untick any you do not want, or tick **New Photos** at the top so only photographs you have not imported before are shown
5. **Right side,** work down the panels:
   - **File Handling:** set Build Previews to **Standard**. Tick **Don't Import Suspected Duplicates**. Tick **Build Smart Previews** if your photographs will live on an external drive, so you can still edit when it is unplugged
   - **Apply During Import:** in **Metadata**, choose **New**. Type your name in Creator and your copyright, such as "© 2026 Your Name", in Copyright. Give it a name such as "My copyright" and click **Create**. In **Keywords**, type a couple of words, such as the place
   - **Destination:** click the folder that will hold all your photographs, such as Pictures, then Photographs. Set **Organize** to **By date** and choose a date format such as 2026/2026-11-23. Folders by date never need rethinking, unlike folders by subject
6. **Bottom middle:** open the **Import Preset** menu and choose **Save Current Settings as New Preset**. Call it "Memory card". Next time, choose it and every setting above is filled in
7. Click **Import** at the bottom right

The photographs appear in the grid as they arrive. Eject the card only when the progress bar at the top left has finished.

## Finding things again

All of these are in the **Library** module.

### Keywords

1. Select the photographs. Click the first, then **Shift-click** the last to select a run, or **Ctrl-click** (Cmd-click) to pick them one by one. **Ctrl+A** (Cmd+A) selects everything showing
2. In the right panels, open **Keywording**
3. Click in the box that says "Click here to add keywords", type words separated by commas, such as "Arran, Glen Rosa, mountains", and press Enter

A few per shoot is enough: place, subject, event.

### Collections

A collection is a group of photographs from anywhere in your library, such as "Autumn competition 2026". A photograph can be in many collections without being copied.

1. Select the photographs
2. In the left panels, find **Collections** and click the **+** at its right
3. Choose **Create Collection**, type a name, tick **Include selected photos** and click **Create**

To add more later, drag photographs onto the collection's name.

A **smart collection** fills itself by rule. Click the same **+**, choose **Create Smart Collection**, and set a rule such as "Rating is 5 stars".

### Searching

In the grid (press **G**), press **\\** (backslash) to show the **Library Filter** bar across the top. Click **Text** to search by keyword or file name, or **Attribute** to show only picks or star ratings.

## Backups

Two separate backups are needed:

1. **The catalog.** Open **Catalog Settings**: on Windows it is in the **Edit** menu; on a Mac, in the **Lightroom Classic** menu. On the **General** tab, set Back up catalog to **Once a week, when exiting Lightroom**. When you close Lightroom on backup day, click **Back Up**
2. **The photographs.** The catalog backup does not include them. Copy your Photographs folder to a second drive, and ideally keep a third copy somewhere else, such as cloud storage or a drive at a friend's house

The Organising and backing up lesson goes into this in more detail.

> If you cannot find a photograph, you do not really have it.
