Adobe makes two Lightroom apps. Lightroom Classic lives on your computer. **Lightroom** (sometimes called Lightroom CC or Lightroom mobile) keeps your photographs in Adobe's cloud and works on a phone, tablet, computer and at lightroom.adobe.com.

The sliders are the same in both, so everything in the editing order lesson applies here too.

## Getting started on a phone

1. Install **Lightroom** from the App Store or Google Play and open it
2. Sign in with the same Adobe account you use for Lightroom Classic
3. To bring in photographs from your phone, tap the **add photos** button (a picture with a +, usually at the bottom right), choose **From Camera Roll** (or Device), select the photographs and tap **Add**
4. You can also turn on **Auto Add** in the app's settings, so every new phone photograph is added automatically

## Using Lightroom on its own

If you mostly shoot on a phone, or want one app everywhere, Lightroom on its own is a good choice:

- Photographs you add are uploaded to the cloud and appear on every device signed in to your account
- Organise them in **albums** (like collections). Tap **+** beside Albums to make one, then add photographs to it
- Search by content, such as "dog" or "beach", without adding keywords
- Cloud storage is limited by your plan, so keep an eye on it if you shoot a lot of RAW

## Using it alongside Lightroom Classic

If Classic is your main library, Lightroom becomes a way to edit and show photographs away from the computer.

### Turning on sync

1. In Lightroom Classic, click the **sync icon** (a cloud) at the top right of the window and choose **Start Syncing**
2. Sign in with your Adobe account if asked. The sync settings are also in Preferences (Settings on newer Macs), on the **Lightroom Sync** tab

### Choosing what to sync

1. In the **Library** module, find your **Collections** in the left panels
2. Right-click a collection and choose **Sync with Lightroom**, or tick **Sync with Lightroom** when you create a new collection. A sync icon appears beside its name
3. On your phone it appears as an album with the same name

Classic sends small previews and your edits, not your full original files, so syncing uses far less cloud space than uploading the photographs themselves.

### What comes back

- Edits made on the phone sync back into the Classic catalog
- Photographs added on the phone download into Classic automatically and appear in the **All Synced Photographs** collection. To choose which folder they land in, open the **Lightroom Sync** tab in Preferences and set **Location**

## Editing on a phone, step by step

1. Open an album and tap a photograph
2. Tap **Edit** (the sliders icon). The panels sit in a row along the bottom; swipe left to see more
3. Work through them in this order:
   - **Crop:** drag the corners, rotate with the dial, tap **Auto** to straighten
   - **Presets** or **Profiles:** choose a starting look, such as Adobe Color
   - **Light:** Exposure, Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Whites, Blacks. Tap **Curve** for the tone curve
   - **Color:** White balance (tap the eyedropper and touch something neutral), Vibrance, Saturation. Tap **Mix** for single colours, **Grading** for tinting
   - **Effects:** Texture, Clarity, Dehaze, Vignette, Grain
   - **Masking:** tap it, then **Subject**, **Sky** or **Background**, or **Brush**, **Linear** or **Radial**. The same sliders appear for just that area. Tap the tick to finish
   - **Detail:** Sharpening and Noise Reduction. Pinch to zoom in first
   - **Optics:** tick Remove Chromatic Aberration and Enable Lens Corrections
   - **Geometry:** Upright, to straighten buildings
4. **Press and hold** the photograph to see the original. **Double-tap** a slider to reset it
5. Tap the undo arrow at the top if something goes wrong

### Copying an edit

Tap the **three dots** at the top right, choose **Copy Settings**, tick what to copy and tap the tick. Open the next photograph, tap the three dots and choose **Paste Settings**.

The names and positions of buttons shift a little between versions and between phones and tablets. If something is not where described, look for its name.
