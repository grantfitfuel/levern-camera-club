Lightroom lets you move any slider in any order, but some steps change what the later ones do. Working in a steady order gives better results and fewer second guesses.

Every edit here is non-destructive. Nothing touches your original file, and the History panel on the left lets you step back to any point.

## 1. Foundations

- **Profile** (top of the Basic panel). Adobe Color is a good start. For RAW files, try the Camera Matching profiles too, which look like the camera's own JPEG
- **Lens Corrections.** Tick Remove Chromatic Aberration and Enable Profile Corrections. This fixes distortion and darkened corners
- **Crop and straighten.** Press **R**. Drag the corners; press **A** to lock or unlock the aspect ratio and **X** to switch between landscape and portrait. The Cropping lesson covers composition

## 2. Tones (Basic panel, top to bottom)

- **White balance.** Press **W** for the eyedropper and click something that should be neutral grey, or use Temp and Tint. The White balance lesson explains why it matters
- **Exposure.** Set the overall brightness, judged on the main subject
- **Contrast.** A little often helps RAW files, which start flat
- **Highlights and Shadows.** Pull highlights down to rescue sky; lift shadows to open up dark areas. Go gently, or the photograph turns grey and flat
- **Whites and Blacks.** Set the brightest and darkest points. Hold **Alt** (Option on a Mac) while dragging to see clipping, or press **J** to show clipping warnings. The Black and white points lesson explains this in detail

## 3. Presence and colour

- **Texture** sharpens fine detail; **Clarity** adds mid-tone punch; **Dehaze** cuts haze. All three go wrong quickly, so keep them low
- **Vibrance** before **Saturation.** Vibrance lifts muted colours and protects skin tones
- **Tone Curve** for fine contrast control, such as a gentle S-curve
- **Color Mixer (HSL)** to adjust single colours, such as darkening a blue sky or calming a loud green
- **Color Grading** to tint shadows, mid-tones and highlights. A little goes a long way. The Colour theory lesson helps here

## 4. Detail and finishing

- **Detail.** Sharpening and noise reduction. Zoom to 100% first. **Denoise** (in the same panel) is very effective on high ISO RAW files. Depending on your version, it may save the result as a separate DNG file next to the original. The Sharpening and noise lesson covers the settings
- **Effects.** Post-crop vignette and grain, if the photograph needs them

## Checking your work

- **\\** (backslash) toggles before and after
- **Y** shows before and after side by side
- Step away for a few minutes, then look again with fresh eyes

When one photograph is right, copy its settings to similar frames: select the edited photograph, then the others, and click **Sync** (or Ctrl+Shift+S / Cmd+Shift+S).

The Editing lightly lesson is worth reading alongside this one.

> Fix it, then finish it. Foundations first, flourishes last.
