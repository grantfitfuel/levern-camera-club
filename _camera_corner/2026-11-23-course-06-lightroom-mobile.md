Adobe makes two Lightroom apps. Lightroom Classic lives on your computer. **Lightroom** (sometimes called Lightroom CC or Lightroom mobile) keeps your photographs in Adobe's cloud and works on a phone, tablet, computer and at lightroom.adobe.com.

The sliders are the same in both, so everything in the editing order lesson applies here too.

## Using Lightroom on its own

If you mostly shoot on a phone, or want one app everywhere, Lightroom on its own is a good choice:

- Photographs you add are uploaded to the cloud and appear on every device
- Organise with **albums** (like collections), stars and flags
- Search works by content, such as "dog" or "beach", without keywords
- Cloud storage is limited by your plan, so keep an eye on it if you shoot a lot of RAW

## Using it alongside Lightroom Classic

If Classic is your main library, Lightroom becomes a way to edit and show photographs away from the computer:

- In Classic, tick the box beside a collection to **sync** it. The cloud receives Smart Previews, not your full files, so it uses no cloud storage
- Edit on your phone or tablet, and the edits sync back into the catalog
- Photographs taken in the Lightroom phone camera, or added on your phone, download into Classic automatically. Choose where they go in Preferences, under Lightroom Sync

## The editing panels

On a phone the panels run along the bottom. Work through them in this order:

1. **Crop** to straighten and frame
2. **Profiles** (on the Edit screen) to choose the starting look
3. **Light**: Exposure, Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Whites, Blacks, and the Curve
4. **Color**: White balance, Vibrance, Saturation and the Color Mixer, plus Grading
5. **Effects**: Texture, Clarity, Dehaze, Vignette, Grain
6. **Masking** for local edits, with the same Subject, Sky and Background selections as Classic
7. **Detail**: Sharpening and Noise Reduction
8. **Optics**: Lens corrections and chromatic aberration
9. **Geometry**: Upright, to straighten buildings

Tap and hold the photograph to see the original. Copy settings from the three-dot menu and paste them onto similar photographs.

The Editing on a phone lesson has more tips for small screens.

> The best editing app is the one you have with you.
