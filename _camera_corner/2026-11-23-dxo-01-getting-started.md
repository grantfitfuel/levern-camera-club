DxO PhotoLab, from a French company, is bought outright rather than rented, with paid upgrades for new versions; check dxo.com for the current editions and a free trial. Its strengths are automatic lens corrections, made from DxO's own lab tests of each camera and lens, and its **DeepPRIME** noise reduction, which is among the best available. Many photographers use it alongside Lightroom just for those.

## No importing needed

PhotoLab works directly on your folders. There is no separate import step: browse to a folder and your photographs are there. Edits are saved alongside them in small sidecar files (ending .dop) and in PhotoLab's own database.

## The two main tabs

Along the top of the window:

- **PhotoLibrary:** browse folders, rate photographs and search. Your folders are listed on the left
- **Customize:** editing. The photograph in the middle, the editing palettes on the right, and a filmstrip of the folder along the bottom

## Your first visit

1. Open **PhotoLibrary**, click your Photographs folder on the left, then a shoot inside it
2. Double-click a photograph, or click **Customize** at the top
3. The first time PhotoLab sees a new camera and lens, it offers to **download the DxO Optics Module**. Click to download it. These modules hold the lens corrections; they are free
4. PhotoLab applies a default set of corrections automatically: lens fixes, a little Smart Lighting and noise reduction. That is the starting point

## Finding your way in Customize

- **Right: the palettes,** grouped as **Light**, **Color**, **Detail**, **Geometry**, **Local Adjustments** and others. Click a palette's heading to open or close it. The small icons at the top of the panel filter the palettes by group, and there is a search box to find a tool by name
- **Top toolbar:** **Compare** (before and after; press and hold), zoom, crop, horizon, repair, and **Local Adjustments**
- **Bottom: the filmstrip.** Click to move between photographs. Stars and picks are set here too
- Each correction has an **on/off switch** beside its name; click it to compare. A curved arrow resets it

## Undo and history

**Ctrl+Z** (Cmd+Z on a Mac) undoes. The **History** palette lists each step. To start again, right-click the photograph and choose to reset corrections (or apply the default preset).

## Ratings

In PhotoLibrary, press **1 to 5** for stars, or use **Pick** and **Reject** under each thumbnail. **Projects** (on the left) group photographs from different folders, like collections.

> Let the lens corrections do the dull work. Then make it yours.
