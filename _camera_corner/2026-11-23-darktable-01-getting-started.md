darktable is free, made by volunteers, and runs on Windows, Mac and Linux. It organises and edits RAW files without touching the originals, much like Lightroom Classic. It is very capable, but it uses different names and a different way of thinking, so expect a learning curve. The official user manual at docs.darktable.org is excellent.

## Getting it

Download it from **darktable.org** (Install section) for your computer and install it as usual. It is updated a couple of times a year; the tips here follow version 5.6.

## Views

darktable is split into **views**, chosen at the top right:

- **lighttable:** browse, rate and organise. Like Lightroom's Library
- **darkroom:** edit one photograph. Like Lightroom's Develop
- Others, such as **map**, **slideshow** and **print**

Double-click a thumbnail in the lighttable to open it in the darkroom. Click **lighttable** at the top right to go back. Press **H** in any view to see its keyboard shortcuts.

## Adding photographs

1. In the lighttable, find the **import** module on the left
2. **add to library:** adds photographs from where they already are, without copying. Use it for folders on your computer
3. **copy & import:** copies photographs off a memory card into a folder you choose, then adds them. Set the folder naming (by date is best) in its options
4. Choose the folder or card, select the photographs and click the **add** or **import** button

Like Lightroom, darktable keeps a library database, plus a small sidecar file (ending .xmp) next to each photograph holding its edits.

## Rating

In the lighttable, hover over a thumbnail (or select it) and press **1 to 5** for stars, **0** to clear, **R** to reject. Colour labels are **F1 to F5**. The bar at the top filters by rating.

## Finding your way in the darkroom

- **Right: the modules.** Each edit is done by a **module**, such as exposure or crop. Modules are grouped by the tabs above them. The **search box** at the top finds any module by name, which is the quickest way for beginners
- **The quick access panel** (the first tab) collects the most common controls in one place
- **Left: the history stack.** Every module you have used, in order. Click an earlier line to go back to that point. **compress history stack** tidies it
- **Bottom: the filmstrip,** and a row of buttons for clipping warnings, soft-proofing and more

## Using a module

- Click a module's name to open it. The **power button** at its left switches it on or off, which is a quick before and after
- **Drag** a slider, or **right-click** it and type a value, then press Enter
- **Scroll** the mouse wheel over a slider for small steps
- **Double-click** a slider's name to reset it
- **Ctrl+Z** (Cmd+Z on a Mac) undoes

> It feels different at first. After a week it feels like home.
