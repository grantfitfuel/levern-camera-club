Most shoots have a handful of strong photographs and many that are nearly there. The skill is spotting the handful quickly, then putting your editing time into them.

## The views

In the Library module:

- **G** grid view, to see the whole shoot at once
- **E** loupe view, one photograph filling the screen
- **C** compare view, two photographs side by side. Useful for near-identical frames
- **N** survey view, several selected photographs together
- **Z** zooms to 100% to check focus

## Flags, stars and colours

- **P** flags a pick, **X** marks a reject, **U** removes the flag
- **1 to 5** give stars, **0** removes them
- **6 to 9** add colour labels, which some people use for "to print" or "for competition"

Turn on **Caps Lock** and Lightroom moves to the next photograph each time you flag or rate. Culling becomes a rhythm: look, press a key, move on.

## A two-pass cull

1. **First pass, quickly.** Go through the shoot in loupe view. Press P for anything with promise and X for anything clearly wrong: missed focus, closed eyes, a duplicate. Do not linger
2. **Second pass, on the picks only.** Filter to flagged photographs (the filter bar at the top of the grid, or the flag icons at the bottom right). Give the best three stars, and the very best five
3. **Compare the close calls.** Select two similar frames and press C. Keep the sharper one, the better expression, the cleaner edge

When you are ready, delete rejects with **Ctrl+Backspace** (Cmd+Delete on a Mac). Lightroom asks whether to remove them from the catalog only or delete them from the disk too.

## Being honest

- Check focus at 100%. A photograph that looks sharp small may not be
- Leave it a day. Your favourite on the day is often the one with the best memory attached, not the best picture
- Judge it as a stranger would. Would it make sense to someone who was not there?

The Organising and backing up lesson covers ratings and collections in more detail.

> You only need to edit the ones worth showing.
