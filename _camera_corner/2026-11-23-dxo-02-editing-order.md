## 1. Geometry

In the **Geometry** palettes:

- **Distortion** and **Vignetting** are usually already corrected by the optics module
- **Horizon:** click the tool in the top toolbar and draw a line along the horizon
- **Crop:** click the crop tool in the top toolbar, choose a ratio from the menu, drag the corners, then press **Enter**
- **Perspective:** fixes leaning buildings. Choose the mode and drag the points onto lines that should be straight. (In some editions this is part of the ViewPoint add-on)

## 2. Light

Open the **Light** palettes:

- **Exposure Compensation:** overall brightness
- **Smart Lighting:** DxO's automatic balancing of highlights and shadows. Choose **Uniform** for most scenes or **Spot Weighted** for portraits (it finds faces), and set the **Intensity**. It is often the most useful single slider in the program
- **Selective Tone:** **Highlights**, **Midtones**, **Shadows** and **Blacks** for fine-tuning
- **Contrast:** **Contrast**, **Microcontrast** (punch in fine detail) and **Fine Contrast**. Keep microcontrast modest
- **ClearView Plus:** cuts haze. Good for distant hills; easy to overdo
- **Tone Curve** for precise contrast

## 3. Colour

- **White Balance:** use the picker and click on something neutral grey, or set **Temperature** and **Tint**
- **Color Rendering:** chooses how colours are translated from the camera. The default (your camera body) is fine; film looks are here too
- **HSL** or **Color Wheel:** change one colour at a time, such as calming a loud green or deepening a blue sky
- **Vibrancy** and **Saturation:** modest amounts
- **Colour grading:** tint shadows, mid-tones and highlights. In version 10 this is a single colour wheel with dots for each range

## 4. Detail and noise

- **Denoising:** choose the method. **HQ** is quick. **DeepPRIME** and **DeepPRIME XD3** (Elite edition) are the AI methods: they take longer but give remarkable results on high ISO photographs. The effect shows only in a small **Correction Preview** window while you edit; it is applied in full when you export
- **Lens Sharpness:** sharpening matched to your lens. The default is good; raise **Global** slightly if needed

## Copying corrections

Right-click the edited photograph in the filmstrip and choose **Copy Correction Settings** (Ctrl+Shift+C, Cmd+Shift+C), select the others and choose **Paste Correction Settings** (Ctrl+Shift+V, Cmd+Shift+V). Save a favourite set as a **preset** from the **Presets** panel.

> The lens corrections are automatic. The decisions are still yours.
