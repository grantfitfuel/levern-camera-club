Lightroom Classic is only as good as its organisation. Ten minutes of setting up now saves hours of hunting later.

## How Classic stores things

Two separate things work together:

- **Your photographs** live in ordinary folders on your computer or an external drive
- **The catalog** (a file ending .lrcat) records where each photograph is, plus every rating, keyword and edit

The catalog does not contain your photographs. If you move or rename folders outside Lightroom, it loses track of them and shows a question mark. Always move and rename from inside Lightroom's Folders panel.

## One catalog

Use one catalog for everything. Several catalogs split your photographs up and make searching harder. A single catalog comfortably holds hundreds of thousands of photographs.

## Importing

Press **Ctrl+Shift+I** (Cmd+Shift+I on a Mac), or click Import at the bottom left of the Library module. At the top of the window choose how the files arrive:

- **Copy** copies photographs from a memory card into a folder you choose. Use this for cards
- **Add** leaves photographs where they already are and adds them to the catalog. Use this for files already on your drive
- **Move** moves files to a new folder. Rarely needed
- **Copy as DNG** converts RAW files to Adobe's DNG format as it copies. Optional; ordinary RAW files are fine

On the right of the import window:

- **Destination:** a main Photographs folder, organised by date, such as 2026 then 2026-11-23. Dates never need rethinking, unlike subject folders
- **Build Previews:** Standard is a good balance. 1:1 previews are slower to build but make zooming in fast
- **Build Smart Previews:** small editable copies, so you can edit when the drive is not connected. Worth ticking if your photographs live on an external drive
- **Don't Import Suspected Duplicates:** tick it
- **Apply During Import:** add a metadata preset with your name and copyright, and a few broad keywords such as the place

Save all this as an import preset at the bottom of the window, so every card imports the same way.

## Finding things again

- **Keywords:** a few per shoot is enough. Place, subject, event. Add them in the Keywording panel in Library, or select a whole shoot and add them at once
- **Collections:** groups of photographs from anywhere in your library, such as "Autumn competition 2026" or "Best of Arran". A photograph can sit in many collections without being copied
- **Smart collections:** collections that fill themselves by rule, such as "five stars from this year"

## Backups

Two separate backups are needed:

1. **The catalog.** In Catalog Settings (Edit menu on Windows, Lightroom Classic menu on a Mac), set backup to once a week, when Lightroom exits
2. **The photographs.** The catalog backup does not include them. Copy your Photographs folder to a second drive, and ideally keep a third copy somewhere else, such as cloud storage or a drive at a friend's house

The Organising and backing up lesson goes into this in more detail.

> If you cannot find a photograph, you do not really have it.
