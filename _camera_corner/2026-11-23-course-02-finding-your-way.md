The other tips say things like "in the Basic panel" or "use the Spot Healing Brush". This lesson shows where those are and how to use them. Keep it open in another window the first few times.

## Getting the apps

All three come with Adobe's **Photography plan**, a monthly or yearly subscription.

1. Sign up at adobe.com and create an Adobe account
2. Install the **Creative Cloud** app it offers you. Open it, find Lightroom Classic and Photoshop in the list and click **Install** beside each
3. On a phone or tablet, install **Lightroom** from the App Store or Google Play and sign in with the same Adobe account

Use the same account everywhere so the apps can talk to each other.

## Lightroom Classic: the layout

The window is split into five areas:

- **Top right: the modules.** Library, Develop, Map, Book, Slideshow, Print, Web. You will mostly use **Library** (organising) and **Develop** (editing). Click a name to switch. Shortcuts: **G** goes to Library, **D** to Develop
- **Left panels.** In Library: your Catalog, Folders and Collections. In Develop: Navigator, Presets, Snapshots and **History** (every change you have made, in order)
- **Right panels.** In Library: Quick Develop, Keywording and Metadata. In Develop: the **Histogram**, a strip of tools under it, then the editing panels: **Basic**, Tone Curve, Color Mixer, Color Grading, Detail, Lens Corrections, Transform, Effects and Calibration
- **Middle.** The photograph, or a grid of them. Under it sits the **toolbar**; press **T** if it is missing
- **Bottom: the filmstrip.** Thumbnails of the photographs you are working on. Click one to open it

### Opening and closing panels

- Click a panel's name, such as **Basic**, to open or close it
- Scroll the right-hand side to reach panels further down
- Right-click any panel name and tick **Solo Mode**. Now only one panel is open at a time, which saves a lot of scrolling
- The small grey triangles at the very edges of the window hide or show a whole side. Press **Tab** to hide both sides, or **Shift+Tab** to hide everything and see just the photograph. Press it again to get them back

### The tool strip in Develop

Just under the histogram is a row of small icons. From left to right they are roughly: **Edit** (the sliders), **Crop** (R), **Remove** (healing, Q), **Red Eye** and **Masking** (Shift+W). Hover over any icon to see its name. Click it once to open it, and again (or press Enter, or click **Close** or **Done** at the bottom) to finish.

### Using a slider

- **Drag** it left or right
- **Click the number** at its right end and type a value, then press Enter
- **Double-click the slider's name** (for example the word Exposure) to reset it
- **Ctrl+Z** (Cmd+Z on a Mac) undoes the last change, as many times as you like
- Click any earlier line in the **History** panel to go back to that point
- **Reset** at the bottom right of Develop undoes every edit on that photograph

### Keyboard shortcuts

Press **Ctrl+/** (Cmd+/ on a Mac) for a list of shortcuts in the module you are in.

## Photoshop: the layout

- **Left: the toolbar.** One icon per tool. Hover to see its name and shortcut letter. A tiny triangle in the corner of an icon means more tools are hidden behind it: **click and hold** (or right-click) to see them
- **Top: the options bar.** It changes to show the settings for whichever tool you have chosen, such as brush size, opacity and **Sample All Layers**
- **Right: the panels.** The most important is **Layers**, usually at the bottom right. If any panel is missing, find it in the **Window** menu, such as Window, Layers. **Window, Workspace, Photography** sets up a sensible layout
- **Properties panel.** Shows the settings for an adjustment layer when you click it

### The Layers panel

Layers stack like clear sheets, the top one in front. Along the bottom of the Layers panel is a row of icons. From left to right: link, **fx** (effects), **add a mask** (a rectangle with a circle in it), **new adjustment layer** (a circle, half black and half white), group (folder), **new layer** (a square with a plus) and **delete** (bin).

- Click a layer to choose it. Anything you do affects only that layer
- Click the **eye** beside a layer to hide or show it
- Drag layers up or down to change their order

### Brushes and painting

- **[** makes the brush smaller and **]** larger. **Shift+[** and **Shift+]** make it softer or harder
- The **Opacity** box in the options bar sets how strong each stroke is. Pressing number keys sets it quickly: 1 is 10%, 5 is 50%, 0 is 100%
- The two colour squares at the bottom of the toolbar are the colours you paint with. **D** sets them to black and white, **X** swaps them

### Moving around the photograph

- **Ctrl++** and **Ctrl+-** zoom in and out (Cmd on a Mac). **Ctrl+0** fits the photograph to the screen
- Hold the **Space bar** and drag to move around while zoomed in
- **Ctrl+Z** undoes, as many times as you like. **Window, History** shows every step

## Lightroom on a phone

1. Tap a photograph to open it
2. Tap **Edit** (the sliders icon) along the bottom. On a tablet the tools are on the right
3. The panels sit in a row: Presets, Light, Color, Effects, Detail, Optics, Geometry and more. Tap one to open it, then slide your finger along a slider
4. **Double-tap** a slider to reset it
5. **Press and hold** the photograph to see the original
6. Undo and redo arrows are at the top of the screen

The order of icons changes a little between versions and between phones and tablets, but the names are the same.

> Nobody remembers where everything is at first. Hovering to read a tool's name is not cheating.
